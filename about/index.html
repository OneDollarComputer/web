About the One Dollar Computer

The One Dollar Computer is more than a microcontroller. It is an open educational hardware project built to lower barriers, awaken talent, and put real engineering tools in anyone’s hands. Official site: onedollarcomputer.com.

Core mission

To awaken talent around the world. Talent is everywhere; opportunity often is not. The One Dollar Computer exists to close that gap — making electronics feel as reachable as a pencil.

What it is

  • A minimal RISC‑V educational computer (~$1 design intent)
  • Designed for extremely low-cost manufacturing
  • WebHID programmable
  • Friendly to schools, clubs, and independent learners
  • Open hardware meant to be fabricated anywhere

Why “One Dollar”?

The name is a declaration of intent: radical accessibility, psychological permission to experiment, and the belief that powerful tools need not be expensive. Even if costs fluctuate, the mission does not.

Designed in Austin. Meant for the world.

Designed in Austin, Texas — intended for classrooms, makerspaces, emerging markets, and anyone who wants to invent. Geography should not decide who becomes an engineer.

Open with responsibility

Anyone can download, manufacture, and improve the design. Openness should still mean accessibility, integrity, simplicity, education-first thinking, and long-term care for the mission.

A foundation, not a gadget

The One Dollar Computer enables learning, experimentation, educational robotics (ROBOX™), modular ecosystems, web-based tools, and a new generation of inventors. It is infrastructure for invention.
